Research

SuperEEG, Brought Up to Date

Python | GPyTorch | github.com/benolivr/supereeg-gpyt

SuperEEG estimates activity throughout the brain from a small number of implanted electrodes. It learns how activity at different locations correlates across many patients, then uses Gaussian process regression to fill in the places nobody recorded. It was my advisor's doctoral project. I ported it to GPyTorch so model building can run on the GPU, moved it to modern Python (3.12+, NumPy 2), and added a tool that converts files saved in the old format.

Multi-Subject Archetypal Analysis of fMRI

First-author paper, in revision at Network Neuroscience | github.com/the-brAIn-lab/msaa-paper

PCA compresses brain data, but its components mix positive and negative weights and are hard to read. Multi-subject archetypal analysis (MS-AA) instead describes each moment as a blend of a few extreme patterns shared across people. I fit it to fMRI from 87 people as they heard a story, heard it word-scrambled, or rested. In held-out participants, 5–10 patterns carried much of the shared signal. The patterns were reproducible, spanned several brain networks and shifted with condition. In simulations, MS-AA recovered the underlying patterns more directly than PCA.

Brain Dynamics After Trauma

Early stage

Next, I'm looking at combining archetypal analysis with timecorr, which measures how brain regions rise and fall together moment by moment, and how that coordination itself shifts. The plan is to apply both to fMRI from trauma-exposed people, to see whether exposure leaves a shared signature in how the brain coordinates itself, and whether people who go on to struggle, stay resilient or recover look different.

Decoding and Identifying Natural Images from fMRI

Python | scikit-learn | NumPy

Two ways to read images out of visual cortex. Ridge regression straight to pixels recovers little more than where a scene is bright; a Gabor encoding model picks the right image out of 120 in 78% of cases, close to the original paper.
